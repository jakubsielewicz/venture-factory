## Sweep scope

Eight candidate ideas across three filter categories:
- **(A)** AU/ANZ regulatory forcing functions where the obvious incumbent is absent or asleep
- **(B)** Boring B2B niches with legacy/enterprise-only incumbents
- **(C)** Sub-segments the big incumbent won't serve

Meta-filter applied: demand alone does not score. Each idea requires BOTH a real demand signal AND an open lane (no nimble, affordable, free incumbent already filling the gap). Per prior scout pattern, ideas with a crowded or fast-moving incumbent land at 56–63 PARK; the open-lane profile produces 70+.

## Ranked shortlist

### Rank 1 — HVNL 2026 Safety Management System (SMS) for Small Heavy Vehicle Operators
**Category:** A — regulatory forcing function, incumbent absent for small operators
**One-liner:** A template-driven, audit-ready Safety Management System platform for small heavy vehicle operators (1–20 trucks) who must comply with mandatory HVNL August 2026 SMS requirements but cannot afford enterprise fleet telematics systems.

**Demand signal:** HVNL reforms commence 1 August 2026 with NO grace period. Every accredited heavy vehicle operator must hold a documented, auditable SMS. The reforms replace NHVAS with a two-tier HVA scheme (GSA for all accredited, ACA replacing BFM/AFM). Chain of Responsibility penalties increase to $10,000 per breach. Accredited operator obligations explicitly expand. (NHVR, August 2026; Netcorp, HVNL Reform Guide, 2026; HSEDIRECT, HVNL Reform 2026.)

**Lane assessment: OPEN (conditionally)**
- Netcorp, Kynection, Logmaster, MTData, Teletrac Navman: all target large or accredited multi-vehicle enterprise operators. Quote-based pricing; no self-serve. Minimum fleet size for most platforms appears to be 10+ vehicles.
- Small operators (1–5 trucks, owner-operator, micro-fleet): explicitly identified as carrying disproportionate compliance burden under the old system. No affordable self-serve SMS builder targeting this segment found.
- FocusIMS (NHVR-focused compliance), HVIM Software, SafetyDocs/NHVASHelp.com.au: template-based document sellers, not SaaS. Gap: no living, auditable SMS platform for sub-20-truck operators.
- Gap: a SaaS tool that generates a compliant, auditable SMS for small operators without requiring a telematics stack purchase. **Lane appears open for the sub-20-truck self-serve segment.** Verify at G1.

**Rough score:** 68–72 (demand strong, forcing function confirmed, lane partially open but needs verification — large incumbents exist but don't serve small operators affordably)
**Verdict:** PURSUE/PARK borderline — top pick for scoring

### Rank 2 — AASB S2 Group 2 Climate Disclosure: Mid-Market Self-Serve Tool
**Category:** A — regulatory forcing function, incumbent is enterprise-only or expensive
**One-liner:** A self-serve AASB S2 / ASRS climate disclosure tool for Group 2 companies (revenue $200M+, assets $500M+, or 250+ employees) that first report July 2026 — positioned below enterprise platforms (Watershed USD $37K–$264K/year, Salesforce Net Zero Cloud USD $48K–$210K/year) and above SME tools that lack NGER/ASRS depth.

**Demand signal:** Group 2 entities commence mandatory AASB S2 reporting for periods beginning 1 July 2026. Group 2 threshold: ≥2 of (revenue $200M+, assets $500M+, 250+ employees). First reports due late 2027. Group 2 is a significantly larger cohort than Group 1 — estimates unavailable from ASIC (marked as G1 unknown); proxy: ASIC oversees ~29,000 reporting entities, of which a material portion are large proprietary companies. (Eco-shaper, AASB S2 Group 2, 2026; Anthesis, ASRS Guide; ASIC Regulatory Guide 280, March 2025.)

**Lane assessment: PARTIALLY OPEN**
- Enterprise lane: Watershed, Salesforce Net Zero Cloud, Cority — USD $37K–$264K/year. Priced out of mid-market.
- AU-native mid-market lane: Carbonly (AU-specific, claims "mid-market pricing" but no published price), NetNada, Avarni, Sumday, Carbonhalo, eco-shaper, Greener, Trace, Unravel Carbon. At least 8–10 AU-native platforms already competing in this space.
- **Lane assessment: CROWDED.** Unlike prior PARKed ideas, the AU carbon accounting software market has attracted multiple AU-native startups specifically targeting mid-market AASB S2 compliance. The market is active and the gap is being filled rapidly.
- The one remaining sub-segment: accountant/CFO tooling that integrates with existing ERP (Xero/MYOB) for the Group 2 company that handles reporting internally without a sustainability team. Not verified.

**Rough score:** 55–62 — PARK (lane is filling; demand is strong but incumbents are moving fast)

### Rank 3 — People at Work (PAW) Replacement: Psychosocial Risk Assessment for SMEs
**Category:** A — regulatory forcing function, government tool closing, incumbents serve enterprise
**One-liner:** A lightweight, affordable, self-serve psychosocial risk assessment and ongoing compliance tool for Australian SMEs (10–200 employees) to replace the government-endorsed People at Work platform, which closes permanently 2 October 2026.

**Demand signal:** People at Work (PAW), the government-endorsed psychosocial risk assessment tool, shuts down 2 October 2026. Used by ~5,000 Australian businesses and 160,000+ workers. Regulators have upgraded psychosocial hazards to enforceable WHS obligations (all states now in force from Dec 2025); NSW Codes of Practice become mandatory benchmarks from 1 July 2026. PAW closure leaves a documented gap for SMEs. (Medium/Marcin Stepien, March 2026; Comcare PAW page; Altius, 2026.)

**Lane assessment: CROWDED (with one sub-segment opening)**
- Clearhead/PsychosocialRiskPulse: $1/user/month for sub-100 employees — cheap, purpose-built PAW replacement, already live.
- ReFresh: VC-backed ($1.3M pre-seed), SOC2, Deel partnership, enterprise-focused.
- FlourishDx: consulting-led enterprise, no published SME price.
- SafetyCulture: free tier + $24/seat/month, large ecosystem.
- Lahebo: GRC platform from $360/month, Australian.
- **Lane largely occupied.** Clearhead/PsychosocialRiskPulse is already filling the cheap-PAW-replacement gap at $1/user/month. The SME lane is not open.
- Remaining sub-segment: trade businesses (builders, electricians, plumbers) who have NO WHS software at all and need the minimal viable psychosocial evidence trail. Not well-served by survey-centric tools. Defensibility unknown.

**Rough score:** 52–58 — PARK (incumbent already moving on the specific gap; trade SME sub-segment unverified)

**Category:** A — regulatory forcing function, fragmented state regimes, no purpose-built tool for small agencies
**One-liner:** A multi-state labour hire licence management tool for small recruitment and staffing agencies (1–50 staff) operating across QLD, VIC, SA, and ACT, where licensing requirements differ per state and SA just expanded to all industries effective January 2026.

**Demand signal:** SA expanded labour hire licensing to all industries from 29 January 2026; agencies have until 29 July 2026 to apply. Victoria's scheme strengthened from 1 June 2026. QLD mandatory since 2018. ACT mandatory. A national framework is under federal consultation. Multi-state small agencies must now track 4 separate licence regimes. (SafeWork SA, 2026; Consumer and Business Services SA, 2026; Labour Hire Authority VIC, 2026.)

**Lane assessment: PARTIALLY OPEN but narrow**
- Access Vincere Evo: built-in QLD/VIC/SA licensing compliance. Full recruitment platform, not a standalone tool.
- The Access Group Labour Hire Software: multi-state compliance built-in. Enterprise recruitment suite.
- HirePOS, Entire OnHire, Astute Payroll: niche labour hire software players, compliance included.
- Bullhorn: mainstream ATS with labour hire compliance features.
- The gap: a standalone, cheap licence-tracking and renewal-alert tool for very small agencies (2–10 person shops) who already use a basic ATS and just need the compliance layer. Not clearly served.
- **Narrow sub-segment gap.** Market is small (estimate: 5,000–10,000 registered labour hire providers nationally, per state registers; number of small agencies without purpose-built software unknown). Not independently verifiable from web research alone.

**Rough score:** 50–56 — PARK (narrow market, partially served by recruitment platforms, sub-segment unverified)

### Rank 6 — NSW Strata Reform Compliance Pack for Self-Managed Schemes
**Category:** A — regulatory forcing function (NSW Strata Schemes Legislation Amendment Act 2025, April 2026 reforms)
**One-liner:** A compliance checklist and document generator for self-managed strata schemes (<20 lots, no professional manager) to meet new NSW strata reform obligations (Initial Maintenance Schedules, capital works fund reviews, six-monthly reporting, mandatory standard forms).

**Demand signal:** NSW strata reforms April 2026 (final phase) require all schemes to use mandatory standard forms for Initial Maintenance Schedules, capital works fund reviews, and periodic maintenance reporting. Financial penalties up to $22,000 for non-compliant owners corporations. (Baron + Associates, April 2026; HenryWilliam.com.au, April 2026; Bartier Lawyers, March 2026.)

**Lane assessment: INCUMBENT DOMINANT BUT SMALL SCHEMES UNDERSERVED**
- StrataMax, MRI Strata Master, Urbanise Strata: enterprise strata management software. Require professional strata manager; not available to self-managed schemes.
- UnitBuddy (unitbuddy.com.au): explicitly targets self-managed strata. Already operating in this segment.
- Strata.community portal: government-linked; provides guidance but not compliance tools.
- The gap: self-managed schemes need a maintenance schedule generator and compliance tracker, not full property management software. UnitBuddy partially fills this — need to verify UnitBuddy's feature set and pricing at G1.
- **Lane: uncertain.** UnitBuddy appears to be in the gap, but whether its feature set covers the new April 2026 obligations is unclear.

**Rough score:** 48–55 — PARK (small market, UnitBuddy may already be filling the gap, regulatory burden on self-managed schemes is real but market size is very small)

### Rank 7 — RTO Compliance Management for Private Providers (<500 students)
**Category:** B/C — boring niche with expensive incumbents; small RTOs underserved
**One-liner:** A lightweight ASQA-aligned compliance management tool for private RTOs with under 500 enrolled students, covering trainer matrix, TAS review, AVETMISS readiness, and annual ADC preparation — priced below the $395–$995/month tier occupied by RTOSafe and Complynce.

**Demand signal:** ASQA audits in 2026 assess against 2025 Standards for RTOs; outcomes-based compliance requiring evidence of quality training and learner outcomes. Annual Declaration on Compliance (ADC) due in 2026. 4,568 registered RTOs in Australia as of Jan 2026 (ASQA, via search). (ASQA, RTO/ESOS Obligations Planners, 2026; ANK Corp, ADC Guide, 2026.)

**Lane assessment: CROWDED but one niche sub-segment**
- RTOSafe: $395/month (Starter), $695/month (Team), $995/month (Organisation). Well-established.
- Complynce: purpose-built RTO compliance layer. Pricing not published but targets mid-size RTOs.
- aXcelerate: full student management + compliance, used by thousands of RTOs.
- eSkilled, Qualticks, SkillsOnQ: established players.
- The gap: sub-$150/month for very small private RTOs (under 200 students) that are overburdened by full-suite pricing. A "minimal viable ASQA compliance pack" for micro-RTOs.
- **Sub-segment gap is real but narrow.** Market of very small RTOs (say 2,000 out of 4,568 RTOs) is plausible but untested. Competition is active. Defensibility is low.

**Rough score:** 48–54 — PARK (incumbents active, sub-segment unverified, low defensibility)

### Rank 8 — Food Safety FSANZ Standard 3.2.2A Evidence Tool for Food SMEs
**Category:** A — regulatory forcing function (Dec 2023 mandatory), incumbent absent for very small operators
**One-liner:** A mobile-first evidence tool helping small food businesses (cafes, restaurants, caterers, food trucks) maintain the mandatory FSANZ Standard 3.2.2A documentation — Food Safety Supervisor certification records, staff training evidence, and critical food safety control substantiation — avoiding health department improvement notices.

**Demand signal:** FSANZ Standard 3.2.2A mandatory from December 2023. Requires three tools: certified FSS on premises, food handler training records for all staff, and evidence of critical food safety controls. Health department inspectors are the enforcement mechanism. Cafes, restaurants, caterers (Class 2 food businesses) all in scope. Market: ~94,000 food service businesses in Australia (IBISWorld, 2025 via search context). (FSANZ Standard 3.2.2A; NSW Food Authority 3.2.2A page; Food Safety Hub guide, 2026.)

**Lane assessment: CROWDED**
- FoodSafetyHub.com.au: guides and templates. Free.
- SafetyCulture: food safety inspection modules, widely used in hospitality.
- Various state-government-provided free templates (VIC Health, NSW Food Authority).
- Multiple food safety management software platforms exist (FoodReady, Safefood360, etc.).
- **Lane closed.** SafetyCulture dominates the mobile food inspection category; free state-government templates fill the documentation gap; multiple food-specific platforms exist. The forcing function is two years old (Dec 2023) and incumbents have responded.

**Rough score:** 42–48 — KILL (stale forcing function, crowded lane, free alternatives dominate)
