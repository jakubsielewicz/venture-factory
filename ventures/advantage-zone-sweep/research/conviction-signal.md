## Verdict: mixed — 1 PASS (at the boundary), 1 WEAK, 1 FAIL

Only **AASB S2 climate-disclosure readiness** clears the ≥60 PASS bar, and only barely (60/100, right at
the threshold — no factor is a 1, so it is not disqualified, but this is not a strong PASS). Its strength
is genuinely differentiated from the pattern that PARKed four prior aged/health-care ventures in this
portfolio: those failed on **buyer-side WTP** (financially-stressed buyers who tolerate but don't pay for
compliance pain); AASB S2's buyers are $50M+ revenue, non-distressed corporates already *paying* — via FTE
hires, $15-60k/yr vendor contracts, and $750k-1.6M Big4 engagements — for exactly this pain. Its weakness is
different: **crowding** (11 named competitors) and a build that is more complex than a pure
entity-resolution/registry-pipeline play. Franchise Disclosure is a real, evidenced pain with the cleanest
build-fit to the operator's specific edge, but the WTP evidence is one inferential step removed from the
actual proposed product (tracking software vs. the legal drafting people are proven to already buy) —
WEAK, not disqualifying, but not a confident PASS either. Modern Slavery is the weakest of the three: no
enforcement yet, no workflow-archaeology signal, and the most crowded field found in the entire sweep.
