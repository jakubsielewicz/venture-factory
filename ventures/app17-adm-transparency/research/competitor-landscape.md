That absence cuts two ways: it could mean a genuinely open packaging gap for a solo operator to own — or it could mean established players have tried a fixed-price productisation and found buyers won't commit to a discrete fee for this (preferring it bundled into hourly/broader privacy retainers, or waiting for OAIC's final guidance, due ~Sept 2026, before locking a fixed scope). No evidence either way was found; treat as genuinely unresolved, not evidence of opportunity.

## The "cheap bolt-on" risk

Two of the three named boutique consultancies (Helios Salinger, and implicitly IIS Partners via its blog) are **already giving away template privacy-policy wording** as lead-generation content. A privacy officer at a cost-constrained mid-market provider has a low-cost path available today: take a competitor's free template paragraph, adapt it lightly, and self-assess scope informally rather than commission a formal system inventory. Given aged-care-sector financial stress (see below), this is a highly plausible modal response for a meaningful share of the addressable buyer base — the underlying legal exposure (a wrong or incomplete disclosure) is real, but the buyer's revealed preference under budget pressure may be "good enough" over "correct."

## Sector financial stress (disconfirming evidence on willingness-to-pay)

The closest prior venture research in this portfolio (a related aged/health-care venture, since removed) cites StewartBrown's Dec 2025 aged-care financial performance survey: **61% of residential aged-care homes ran at an operating loss in H1 FY26**, average deficit ~$1.04M/home. That venture (same buyer segment, same claimed "Remediant" channel) PARKed at 56/100 partly because "providers are under cost pressure and have been burned by technology promises before... closing a deal in this environment will require longer sales cycles." The same structural buyer-side friction applies directly to this venture: a $4,500 discretionary compliance-documentation spend, positioned as "for your own legal review" rather than a hard legal requirement in itself, competes against real budget constraint in the same buyer population.

## Deadline-driven-consulting failure mode

**This looks structurally like a one-shot sprint unless something makes the register a genuinely re-billable artefact.** Reasoning:
- The core deliverable (inventory workshop + register + drafted policy sections) has a natural "done" state once the Dec 2026 disclosure is live. There is no obvious mechanism forcing repeat engagement absent one of the triggers below.
- **Plausible durability triggers, none yet validated with evidence:**
  1. **New systems trigger re-disclosure.** Any new ADM tool a client deploys after Dec 2026 should, in principle, prompt a policy update — but this is a small, infrequent, low-fee touch-point (an update, not a new inventory), unlikely alone to sustain a business.
  2. **Annual privacy-policy review cycles.** Many mid-market orgs already run an annual compliance/policy review as part of broader governance (ISO, aged-care Quality Standards); the ADM register could be folded into that cadence — plausible but unproven; no evidence found that any org currently treats ADM specifically as part of an annual cycle (the obligation doesn't exist yet).
  3. **OAIC audit/enforcement response work.** If OAIC challenges a client's policy post-commencement, having a maintained register is valuable evidence — but this is reactive, unpredictable, and only applies to the subset of clients who get audited.
  4. **Cross-client ADM-pattern dataset.** The operator's stated long-term asset. Currently aspirational — it requires a critical mass of clients before it has any value, and no evidence was found that any buyer would pay a premium for pattern benchmarking specifically (as distinct from their own inventory).
- **Net read:** the demand curve most plausibly spikes in the ~6-12 months before/around 10 Dec 2026 and decays sharply afterward unless one of the above triggers is actively engineered into the offer (e.g., an annual "re-attestation" retainer sold at the point of initial engagement, priced and named explicitly, not left implicit). This should be treated as a design requirement for G1, not assumed to emerge naturally.
