# Venture brief — cyber-defence-au

> The living source of truth for this venture. **The venture-orchestrator is the sole writer.**
> Specialists propose changes in their own folder; the orchestrator integrates them here.

## One-liner
Low-touch cybersecurity software/service that helps Australian small-to-mid businesses meet baseline cyber-defence expectations (Essential Eight-aligned) without hiring a security team.

## Current state
- **Gate:** G1 (G0 green; G1 validate not yet started)
- **Status:** in-progress
- **Last updated:** 2026-07-20

## Thesis
Australian SMEs face rising cyber-incident exposure, tightening insurer/regulatory expectations (Essential Eight, Privacy Act reform, mandatory breach reporting), and no in-house security capability. If there is a real, evidenced, underserved demand pocket for an affordable, low-touch cyber-defence product/service (vs. enterprise-grade or MSSP-priced offerings), this could be a durable, low-ops revenue product. G0 exists to test that thesis with evidence, not assumption.

## Decisions (dated, append-only)
- 2026-07-20 — Opened venture `cyber-defence-au` to scout the "cyber defence for AU" theme — rationale: rising AU regulatory/insurer pressure on SME cyber posture is a plausible underserved-demand thesis worth a G0 evidence pass.
- 2026-07-20 — G0 scout complete: Opportunity Score 65/100, verdict **PARK** (not PURSUE, not KILL) — demand is real and paid (conviction PASS 64/100) but the specific AU SME low-touch lane is already held by an entrenched incumbent (Cynch Security, 2,000+ paying customers, 9-year head start), so an undifferentiated entrant does not clear the PURSUE bar. Carrying to G1 only if a differentiated wedge can be identified; otherwise this is a KILL candidate at G1.

## Load-bearing assumptions
- AU SMEs feel real, dated pain around cyber-defence compliance/insurance requirements — **verified** (G0): ACSC 2024-25 threat report, average AU small-business incident loss $56,600 (up 14% YoY); 60% of SMEs cite cost as a barrier despite 80% rating cyber security important (ACSC 2023 survey); insurers applying ransomware sub-limits/declining cover absent Essential-Eight-aligned controls.
- Existing options (MSSPs, enterprise GRC tools, consultants) are priced/scoped above what SMEs will pay — **partially falsified** (G0): Cynch Security already serves this exact segment self-serve at $149-799/mo with 2,000+ AU SME customers — the "no affordable self-serve incumbent" assumption does not hold. vCISO retainers ($3.5k-$12k/mo) remain out of reach for SMEs, and a fragmented quote-only MSP "Essential Eight as a Service" tier has unprobed pricing.
- A software-led (vs. services-heavy) wedge can serve this segment at acceptable margin without becoming a high-touch security consultancy — unverified, pending G1 moat/regulatory assessment.
- A differentiated wedge exists against the Cynch incumbent (insurer-integration timing, Privacy Act reform timing, mid-tier pricing, or vertical narrowing) — unverified, the central open question for G1.

## Open risks
- Regulatory/compliance framing may require licensed security or legal expertise the venture cannot provide as decision-support only — owner: domain-advisor at G1.
- "Passive"/low-touch framing may not hold if the product implies ongoing security monitoring or incident response duties — owner: venture-orchestrator, reassess at G1.
- Category has a well-capitalised, entrenched incumbent (Cynch Security — 2,000+ AU SME customers, 9-year head start, Telstra/Victorian Government/KPMG partnerships) directly occupying the low-touch self-serve lane this thesis assumed was open — owner: domain-advisor/growth-marketer at G1, to test for a defensible wedge before G2.
- Bottom-up capture-rate estimate for a new entrant (~$107k-358k ARR over 12-24 months from a ~300,000-business AU SME pool) is an unsourced modelling estimate, not evidenced — flagged as a G2-blocker; owner: financial-analyst at G1.
- Regulatory tailwind (Privacy Act small-business exemption removal) is **not yet legislated** — a wedge that leans on its timing carries legislative-timeline risk — owner: domain-advisor at G1.
