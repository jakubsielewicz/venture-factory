## 1. Firmographics

| Attribute | Definition |
|-----------|-----------|
| Geography | Australia (all states and territories; federal Fair Work Act applies nationally) |
| Size | 1–20 employees; the sweet spot is 3–15 (below ~3 the owner often knows the award intuitively; above ~20 they tend to have part-time HR help) |
| Revenue | AUD $500K–$5M/yr (owner-managed; no dedicated HR budget) |
| Industries (priority) | Hospitality (cafes, restaurants, bars), Retail (independent stores), Childcare and Early Education, Construction (trades and builders), Hair and Beauty, Professional Services firms employing admin/support staff |
| Payroll tooling | Uses Xero, MYOB, or a spreadsheet for payroll; no integrated HR platform |
| Compliance posture | Award-covered staff (at least some employees on a modern award); owner-managed payroll, not outsourced to a specialist payroll bureau |
| Legal structure | Pty Ltd or sole trader with employees; not incorporated association or government entity |

**Why these sectors first:** The 12 awards in the MVP set (Hospitality General Award, General Retail Award, Clerks Award, Fast Food Award, Restaurant Industry Award, Children's Services Award, Hair and Beauty Award, Building and Construction On-site Award, Manufacturing Award, Professional Employees Award, Security Services Award, Miscellaneous Award) cover the employment-sector distribution of the estimated ~593,000 award-covered SMEs (source: ABS Counts of Australian Businesses, August 2025 release; 60% award-coverage rate applied — estimate). These sectors together represent the highest-frequency misclassification risk and the strongest FWO audit activity (FWO Annual Report 2024–25).

## 2. Buyer vs User

| Role | Description | What They Care About |
|------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Economic buyer** | The business owner or director | Avoiding criminal prosecution (Fair Work Act s 327A, operative 1 Jan 2025; up to AUD $8.25M per offence for body corporates); reducing the time cost of manual checking; having a document to show if audited |
| **Day-to-day user** | The business owner themselves, or an office manager / administrator who handles payroll | Speed, clarity of output, not needing to understand award technicalities |
| **Referral influencer** | The bookkeeper, BAS agent, or accountant who manages the client's payroll and wants a defensible check before each year-end | Credibility of output; PDF they can attach to client file |

In micro-businesses (1–5 employees), buyer and user are the same person. In 6–20 employee businesses, the owner is the buyer and an office manager may be the user.

## 3. Buying Trigger

The purchase decision is event-driven, not perpetual. There are three trigger moments:

**Trigger 1 — The July Annual Wage Review (highest frequency, lowest intent lag)**
The FWC Annual Wage Review raises minimum award rates every July (3.5% effective 1 July 2025; 4.75% effective 1 July 2026 — source: Squire Patton Boggs, squirepattonboggs.com, 2026). Every award-covered employer must update pay rates by the first full pay period after 1 July. Owners who miss this create a retroactive underpayment liability. Buying intent peaks in May–July each year.

**Trigger 2 — The Criminal Liability Wake-Up Call**
Fair Work Act s 327A (operative 1 January 2025) made intentional wage underpayment a criminal offence carrying up to AUD $8.25M per offence for body corporates or AUD $1.65M and 10 years imprisonment for individuals (source: Addisons, Commoner Law, Williamson Barwick, 2024–2025 — secondary sources citing Closing Loopholes Act 2023; penalty units at current Commonwealth rate of $330/unit). Many SME owners learn of this via their accountant, a trade association newsletter, or a media story about an FWO prosecution. This is a one-time discovery event that creates immediate intent.

**Trigger 3 — An FWO Audit Notice or Industry Peer Story**
If the owner receives an FWO inquiry, or hears that a competitor or peer was audited and found underpaying, buying intent becomes urgent. This is the highest-conversion trigger but smallest volume.

## 5. Willingness-to-Pay Read

**What they pay for adjacent tools today:**
- Xero: AUD $36–$115/mo (source: Xero website; standard tiers for AU SMEs)
- Employment Hero minimum: AUD $100/mo for HR Essentials at $10/employee (source: employmenthero.com/pricing, fetched 2026-06-18)
- Deputy minimum: AUD $30/mo (source: deputy.com/au/pricing, fetched 2026-06-18)
- FairWork Mate Business plan: from AUD $499/mo (source: fairworkmate.com.au, fetched 2026-06-18)
- Accountant time for a manual compliance check: AUD $150–$300/hr; a thorough check takes 2–4 hours = AUD $300–$1,200 per annual review (estimate — representative range)

**Budget owner:** The business owner. For SMEs using a bookkeeper, the bookkeeper may recommend the tool but the owner approves the subscription. This is a discretionary spend decision made against a monthly SaaS budget that typically allows $50–$200/mo for productivity tools.

**Price sensitivity:**
The Starter tier (AUD $49/mo) is positioned below the psychological threshold where a small business owner expects a sales conversation. At $49/mo they will self-serve. The Pro tier (AUD $79/mo) is the value maximisation tier for 6–20 employee businesses — it costs less than one hour of accountant time per month.

**Willingness-to-pay ceiling for the micro-SME segment:** Evidence from competitor pricing suggests the SME market sustains $49–$99/mo for a standalone compliance-check tool. Employment Hero's $100/mo minimum is cited by competitive teardown as the lower bound of the full-suite segment. FairWork Mate at $499/mo is well above the micro-SME tolerance. The $49–$79 band is a plausible unoccupied niche, but this is an estimate — direct buyer interview validation (10–15 SME owner conversations) is the pre-build gate before committing to this price (per prd.md section 7 and advisory/go-no-go-memo.md).

**Primary financial justification the buyer uses:**
- Avoiding a single FWO penalty (minimum AUD $16,500 for civil underpayment of one employee; criminal liability potentially in the millions) justifies years of subscription cost
- Replacing manual annual award review (2–4 hrs @ accountant rates = $300–$1,200) with a $49/mo subscription

## 6. Anti-ICP

Do not target:

| Segment | Why |
|---------|-----|
| Businesses with 0 employees (sole traders with no staff) | No award compliance obligation; no payroll to check |
| Large enterprises (200+ employees) | Yellow Canary, Employment Hero Enterprise, and dedicated payroll compliance firms already serve this segment; the product's 12-award MVP scope is insufficient for complex multi-award large employers |
| Businesses already on Employment Hero HR or KeyPay | Award interpretation is built into their existing stack; switching cost is high; they are not a winnable prospect at MVP |
| Businesses operating entirely on Enterprise Agreements (EAs) | EAs supersede modern awards; these employers have no modern-award pay-rate obligation to check |
| Bookkeeping / payroll bureaus wanting to resell (at launch) | The Pro tier is available to bookkeepers for their own practice use, but a formal white-label/reseller program is not in the MVP scope; targeting resellers creates support complexity |
| Employers in industries outside the 12 MVP awards | The tool cannot produce a reliable audit report for awards outside the initial set; mis-selling to them risks incorrect output and reputational damage |
