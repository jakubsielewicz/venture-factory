# Sub-framing Shortlist — aml-tranche2

Three sub-framings of the core concept, ranked by attractiveness for a solo/small team.

## Framing A — Cross-profession full-stack platform (all Tranche 2 professions)
**Rank: 2**

Serve all six Tranche 2 profession types (lawyers, accountants, conveyancers, real estate agents, dealers, trust/company SPs) with a single configurable platform.

Pros: Maximum TAM. One build serves all verticals. Cross-profession firms (e.g., accounting/legal) can use one product.

Cons: Extremely crowded space. InfoTrack, AML Shield, ClearAML, AMLTranche are already here. Professional-body channel partnerships are already being claimed by incumbents. No clear differentiation without significant investment in profession-specific templates and workflows for each of six verticals.

Verdict: The market is real but this framing competes head-on with 8+ incumbents. Requires either lower price (hard to sustain) or better features on a low budget. Low first-mover advantage remaining.

---

## Framing B — Real estate agents + dealers specialist platform (underserved segment)
**Rank: 1 (RECOMMENDED)**

Focus specifically on real estate agencies and dealers in precious metals/stones/products — the two Tranche 2 segments not well-served by InfoTrack (whose legal focus is dominant) or TrustEasy (accounting-only). Build deep workflows for property transaction CDD (buyer/seller/BPAY risk, source of funds) and dealer transaction records. Win via REIA, REIQ, REIV, and industry dealer association partnerships before incumbents establish those channels.

Who pays: Principal/licensee of a real estate agency (~44,000 businesses), or a precious metals dealer (~1,000–3,000 businesses). Budget holder is the business owner.

Why low-ops: SaaS; profession-specific templates reduce support load; AUSTRAC's published guidance provides the compliance framework so the product does not need to author novel legal interpretation.

Why it could win: Real estate agents have property transaction CDD complexity (beneficial ownership of trusts, buyer identity across multiple parties) that generic platforms handle poorly. Dealers have unique thresholds (cash transactions >$10,000 TTR requirements; high-value goods risk profiles). Neither InfoTrack (LEAP-focused, law/conveyancing) nor TrustEasy (accounting) targets this vertical.

Risk: Smaller total segment than lawyers/accountants. REIA state body partnerships take time and may require referral fees.

## Framing C — Compliance-officer-as-a-service for micro-firms (1–3 person practices)
**Rank: 3**

A managed compliance subscription where a human compliance expert is backed by software — the firm pays $2,500–$4,000/year and outsources the compliance officer function entirely. Software + human hybrid.

Pros: Higher ARPA than pure SaaS. Differentiates from DIY tools. Addresses the pain point that the smallest firms (sole practitioner lawyers, one-partner accountants) do not want to learn AML compliance at all.

Cons: NOT passive/low-ops — requires human compliance experts per account. Scales poorly. Labour cost makes unit economics tight at <$3,000 ARPA. Directly conflicts with the factory's "low-ops after build" requirement. Also, some platforms (Arctic Intelligence, AML Assist) already offer a human+software hybrid.

Verdict: Fails the passive-fit criterion. PARK unless scoped as a future upsell rather than core product.
